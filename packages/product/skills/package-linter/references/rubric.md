# Package quality rules

The program assigns the calculated features and the coefficient A. The agent makes only the fixed semantic decisions, with a rationale and evidence addresses. First recover the meaning of the whole connected package, then decide for each module and separately for the package. The intermediate file format is in [assessment.md](assessment.md).

## Observations and applicability

Each own WorkFlow is a separate screen. Notes that are empty after trim are excluded from density and from the share of short notes. Independent roots, unknown roles, and a lack of observable content are handled by the rules below.

## Structure: three binary decisions, maximum 3

- `modularity`: a suitable split. The point is forbidden for `flat_large`, and also when there are at least 25 elements and no submodels in the single independent nonempty root context.
- `balance`: how complexity is spread. The point is forbidden when there is at least one critical screen or two overcrowded screens. The agent judges a disproportionate concentration on one screen by comparing it with the other screens and explains it. The share of elements is not a universal ban by itself: one small root is not bad because its own share is one hundred percent.
- `depth`: whether nesting is justified. The point is forbidden when depth is greater than 5, when there are two or more suspicious small submodels, or when depth is zero with at least 25 elements in the single independent nonempty root context.

Each true gives 1, each false gives 0. Null means the decision cannot be made, not a score of zero. An overcrowded screen has 25–39 elements, a critical screen has 40 or more. `flat_large`: the total is at least 25 and at most one screen holds at least 70% of the total.

Count real own WorkFlow screens. Roots of different modules are independent; catalogue definitions are counted once, and each call is a separate element. Count ordinary nodes with a materialized Guid, excluding service nodes, visualizers, and explicit deletions. Useful fields of an inherited record with a Guid are visible, but incompleteness is noted. A raw record without a Guid does not prove that an element exists.

The two bans "many elements without submodels" and "many elements at zero nesting" apply only to one independent nonempty root context. Several independent small roots must not be added into one large flat scenario. An independent context starts at an own WorkFlow with no parent scope, including a standalone catalogue definition; empty roots are not counted. Lifting those two bans does not automatically award the semantic points: the agent still judges whether the split and the depth are suitable. The other bans and the calculated coefficient do not change.

A small submodel has at most 2 direct children, no nested submodels among them, and recursive content without ports of at most 4. Linear and isolated roles are suspicious signs; a join, a branch, a source, or a terminal result justifies the boundary. Confirmed reuse also justifies a shared model. Suspicion is an observation, not proof that something is unnecessary and not an order to delete it. An unconfirmed purpose of an isolated element remains a limitation. If the active links of an inherited owner are unavailable, its role is unknown: that is not isolation and not a reason for a penalty. A catalogue definition with no visible owner and no confirmed calls also does not prove an isolated instance; the role is marked unknown, without a penalty and without the matching depth ban.

A = clamp(1 − Pmax − min(.3, .15 × critical) − min(.15, .05 × overcrowded) − .2 × flat_large − .1 × too_deep − min(.15, .03 × suspicious_small), 0, 1).

Pmax: below 25 is 0; from 25 to 40 is `(maximum−25)/15×.2`; from 40 is `min(.4, .2+(maximum−40)/80×.2)`. By this formula 40 gives .2, 80 gives .3, and 120 gives .4.

## Notes: two binary decisions, maximum 2

- `adequacy`: enough for the size and the important stages. In a small scenario, even the absence of notes is allowed and earns this point. In a large scenario with no notes, or with density below .1, the point is forbidden. At density .1–.2 the agent must explain whether the important stages are covered: the number does not replace reading. From .2, adequacy is usually acceptable, but the usefulness of the coverage is still checked. The share of short notes must be strictly below .5.
- `substance`: substance after reading the text. Notes explain actions, parameters, the meaning of the data, or the choice of approach. Repeating a node type, generic captions, and templates without context do not earn the point. With no notes, substance is false. If clear and empty notes are mixed, the decision needs a rationale by important stages and owners.

Notes whose text is empty after trim are not counted. A large scenario has at least 15 elements; a short note has fewer than 20 characters after trimming spaces. Note owners and nested areas count in the semantic decision; one note on the root does not mean every stage is covered.

A = clamp(1 − penalty, 0, 1). When the element count is zero the formula returns 1, but applicability is separate from the arithmetic: without observable ordinary content the notes direction is unavailable. An empty WorkFlow does not by itself give a numeric quality score. If there are no notes but there is content: penalty .4 for a large scenario, .15 for a small one. Otherwise, for a large scenario: density <.1 adds .25, <.2 adds .15, <.3 adds .05; above that there is no penalty. Then a short-note share >.5 adds .15, and >.3 adds .05. The boundaries are strict.

## Node labels: a category, maximum 2

`good` → 2: most nodes have specific meaningful labels; isolated weaknesses do not get in the way. `systemic` → 1: generic labels, missing extensions, and default labels occur regularly, but naming work is visible. `critical` → 0: most node labels match the type name, a file node has the default label, or most changed labels do not explain the purpose. A majority is strictly more than half. A machine confirmation that most labels are the default, or that a file node has that label, forbids another category.

A good label explains a concrete action, subject, or result in the context of the task. One operation type, transliteration without a subject, a generic category, or a repeat of the type without new meaning is weak. A file-node label must include the file name with its extension. In the semantic conclusion, identical labels of reference nodes are not automatically a defect; judge their role and whether they can be told apart in context.

A default label is an exact match of DisplayName and the type name after trim. The type comes from the bundled static catalogue or a safe own declaration. DisplayName is separate from the service Name. An unknown type or an automatically formed label is not guessed: if A cannot be counted reliably, the direction is unavailable.

A = clamp(1 − min(system_share/.20, 1)×.4 − .2×unrenamed_file − .1×missing_extension − .1×duplicates − .1×partial_repeats − .1×too_short, 0, 1).

A file operation is one whose type name contains "import", "export", or "file". Extension: `\.\w{2,5}$`. A short label has fewer than **5** characters. A partial repeat is when at least half of the type's unique words appear in the label. These three checks run for nodes whose label was changed. The calculated duplicate detector compares exact node labels after the change inside the own module, including different nested areas and reference nodes. The package checks duplicates separately in each module and does not mix independent roots. An exception for justified repeats of reference nodes belongs to the semantic category and the recommendations; it does not change the calculated formula. Do not call a justified repeat a defect in the user text.

## Variable names: three binary decisions, maximum 3

- `style`: one consistent style. The program detects camelCase, snake_case, and PascalCase; the agent must accept the flag. One lowercase word is compatible with camelCase and snake_case, but not with PascalCase. An uppercase abbreviation is compatible with any style. If there is no explicit style, the style is undefined and there is no conflict. On a tie, the first explicit style encountered wins. One variable automatically satisfies this feature.
- `labels`: label language as calculated by the program. It checks that each label contains a Cyrillic letter and that the share of such labels is at least .70. Do not change that result from a visual impression.
- `meaning`: whether the name and label together are understandable for most variables. An informative label can explain a generic or numbered name. A weak name is judged on its own only when the label does not help. One-letter Latin names and the service patterns `var` plus digits, `tmp`, `field` plus digits, and `obj` are always poor for expression references; a clear label does not repair them. When half or more of the names are like that, the semantic point is forbidden. Ordinary technical abbreviations inside an understandable name are fine. The agent checks that the pair matches the type: a logical flag, a count or sum, text, and a date must be distinguishable from the name and the label. When the label is empty, repeats the name, or is itself too generic, the name must explain that role on its own.

A = clamp(1 − .5×style_violation_share − .5×non_cyrillic_share, 0, 1). The coefficient does not judge meaning; that is a separate agent decision. Observable declarations with one source address are not duplicated when boundary sockets are shown. Variable values and expressions are not exported. Without observable declarations the direction is not scored: an empty set is not given a neutral semantic point.

## Formulas and availability

For each available direction, `Q=100×(A+S/M)/2`, where M is fixed at 3/2/2/3. Semantic true gives one, false gives zero; the node category gives 2/1/0. There are no halves and no arbitrary agent points. If there is no A, or at least one required component cannot be judged, the whole direction is unavailable. Components are not removed from M; the 50:50 proportion does not change. If the whole visible part is judged but the XML does not reveal everything, partial is allowed with an explicit coverage rationale.

The total is `T=Σ(w×Q)/Σ(available w)`, with weights 40/25/17.5/17.5. The package gets its own decisions about the overall picture, not the average of the module scores. All arithmetic is Decimal with no intermediate rounding; the printed value is half-up to hundredths. The shown weights and contributions are reconciled by the largest-remainder method. Levels from the unrounded total: [0,30) Unsatisfactory, [30,50) Satisfactory, [50,70) Good, [70,85) Excellent, [85,100] Outstanding. When every direction is unavailable, there is no final number and no level.

The check examples are abstract only: a fictional direction with A=.4 and S=M gives Q=70; A=1 and S=0 gives Q=50. Those numbers illustrate the formula; they do not calibrate a real package.

## Domain guidance for semantic decisions

These are extra guides for reading the subject task, not extra numeric rules. They apply to the existing decisions of the four directions; they do not add points, automatic penalties, or required transformations. Confirmed `forced` values and calculated facts outrank a semantic reading. A useful boundary does not lift a ban, and clean flags do not by themselves prove quality. Every basis is limited to what can be read from the package; unavailable settings and external dependencies are not guessed. It is not required to identify every external consumer or the full schemas: when the visible part has a complete set of decisions, keep `partial`; `null` or `unavailable` is needed only when a specific required decision is impossible or A is missing under the rubric. General unknown does not automatically make the whole structure unavailable. Applying these rules does not require another skill.

### Structure: responsibility and result

- For `modularity`, start from the consumer: what it receives, what a row or element means, which conditions are already met, which inputs are needed, and who uses the output. From that result, set the stage's responsibility. Distinguish data preparation, business use, and delivery when those are real roles. Many modules or submodels do not by themselves prove a separation of responsibility.
- A module is justified by a large independent responsibility and a gain in load and navigation, counting the cost of transitions and interfaces. Local processing often needs only a submodel. One module can be suitable; ROOT should help the reader see the large areas and the results. Do not propose new modules only to increase the count.
- For `balance`, compare real roles and stage complexity, not equal element counts. A composite area with one purpose may contain unequal branches. For `depth`, explain the benefit of nested boundaries: understanding the result, reuse, or a necessary contract. An empty wrapper does not prove decomposition; a small terminal or shared submodel can be useful. These rationales do not lift the rubric bans on screens, depth, and suspicious small submodels.
- Apply only the thresholds and limits in this rubric. Do not add automatic requirements to reshape the scenario. A wide interface of an existing atomic operator is not a semantic defect by itself. Do not mix ports and columns: a known count and kinds of ports do not prove the composition and types of every column.
- Check the whole available context of demand: tables, variables, control and execution order, calls, writing and later reading of files, export, manual review, and publication. A missing visible line does not prove that something is unused. A confirmed shared finished result may be given to several consumers; separate algorithm calls with different parameters are not replaced by one precomputed table. When the role is unknown, keep the limitation instead of concluding that something is unnecessary.
- Check whether an existing meaningful stage result is available to its consumer or for review. Data and a completion signal are different: an export without a data output can be a suitable action, and its completion is not a table for review. If a need to review is confirmed and a prepared result is visible, recommend making that result available while keeping the execution order. Do not require the export to grow a new data output, and do not invent a result, a schema, or a new port. The absence of a visible result does not by itself add a scoring ban.
- Every recommendation preserves data, calculations, settings, schemas, control, and public contracts. Do not propose cloning an import or calculations, or adding intermediate files for the sake of metrics. Recommend deletion or moving to an archive only when something is proven unnecessary after every consumer is considered; unknown demand stays unknown.

### Node labels: whether the label is true

- For `naming_category`, compare the node label with its own available settings, fields, condition, and data order. One operation type or a neighboring label does not prove the meaning. A label that claims chronological freshness needs a confirmed time order; taking the last input value does not prove that. If the settings are not disclosed, distinguish a confirmed error from an inability to check; do not invent the correct label and do not require a rename without a basis.
- Judge repeated names inside the real scenario and role: justified reference nodes and independent operations that share a name are not an automatic semantic defect. The calculated detector A still compares labels inside the whole own module, including nested areas. Justifying a repeat does not change A. Treat case and spaces as cosmetics, not as an automatic semantic penalty.

### Notes: explaining what matters

- For `adequacy` and `substance`, check whether the notes reveal the purpose, input, and result of important stages, the material conditions, parameters, and exceptions, and the reason for a boundary or a choice of approach. Length and density do not replace content. Do not require a note on every obvious node; the adequacy rule for a small scenario and the other rubric limits remain.
- Treat the author's caveats and TODOs as open questions when the available information shows they are still current. Do not turn the author's intention into a completed check or an established business conclusion. Do not ask to describe an unavailable formula as if it were already known.

### Variables: role and required names

- For `meaning`, read the name, the label, and the type together with the variable's role. Keep the rubric limits for service names; the `style` and `labels` rules and every counter also apply to required component parameters and public names. A required name is not an exception from the score.
- Distinguish the score from a safe action. Do not recommend renaming a required component parameter or a public name only for style. Where it is allowed, suggest explaining the role with a label or a note. For a name the author controls, a rename must update every use and keep the contract. If the dependencies or the freedom to change are unknown, state the condition and the limitation instead of an unconditional rename.

A recommendation ties a confirmed weakness to the matching score direction and a concrete change: what to do and how, with the condition needed to keep the rest intact. Reflect unknown as a limitation. Do not promise an exact score increase and do not fill sections with advice for the sake of a count. These detailed checks stay internal analysis; the user receives short plain conclusions in the required format.

## Semantic checklist

This checklist clarifies how to apply the existing decisions. It does not add decisions or points.

- Even when the machine flags are clean, check whether the name reveals a concrete subject, action, or result. Mentally attach it to another step: if the meaning does not change, the label may be too generic. For "Execute node", a precise analytic term is allowed. Do not call justified identical names of reference nodes a defect.
- When reading notes, check whether they explain actions, parameters, the meaning of the data, and the reasons for the approach. Length and density do not replace content. If the explanations are thin, first check the visible formulas, nontrivial conditions, parameters, and the purpose of submodels. Do not require an explanation of something the file does not contain.
- Judge a variable's name and label together with its type. Justify a recommendation about overall style by consistency; justify awkward expression references by service names. Those are different bases and must not be substituted for each other.
- In the conclusion, collapse the same problem into one item at its real scale. Do not spread one example across the whole package. Set priority by the effect on understanding and shared dependencies. Do not add remarks or recommendations to fill a section. Collapse a repeated weakness into one general recommendation at the right shared level; do not list every object and do not repeat the advice in module cards. Keep specific for a different action or an exception. One block is one action: which weakness belongs to which score direction, what to change, and how to make the change. A general recommendation must also be practical. "Clarify", "look", or "check" alone is not enough; name the change and the condition when one is needed. Unknown remains a limitation. Do not promise an exact increase without a new assessment; recommendations for every direction or module are not required.
- Use the ordinary words scenario, submodel, node, label, and port. Start a recommendation with a concrete action. Leave numbers in the score tables and the summary; do not repeat the metrics in the explanations.

The absence of observable variables does not prove a cause and is not a reason to recommend turning off autosynchronization.

## Document language

Write every explanation in plain English. Do not reveal internal fields, XML addresses, hashes, file sizes, versions or models, commands, or agent protocols. In format-3 decisions, evidence supports the conclusion, scope explicitly separates general from specific, and targets set only the exact targets of a concrete action. Scope stays an internal distinction: recommendations are shown as one list by importance, without literal category headings. Each recommendation is a short finished text without steps, one item of a flat list, with no nesting and no separate address fields. The agent writes the nearest submodel and node, or another subject object, into the recommendation itself, putting names in «guillemets» so they become code. The current module is not repeated inside its section. The context must distinguish objects that share a name, but must not build chains with `/`; a `/` inside a name is kept. The program does not append a region from evidence or targets: they stay internal and are checked independently; unresolved targets are rejected. The agent checks clarity and distinguishability in the finished document. Describe a repeated problem with one general recommendation, without listing every node; specific is needed only for a different action or an exception. Findings print only the plain text; a guess is stated honestly by the author, and priority and confidence stay internal. Tables and positive descriptions do not need technical addresses. Machine addresses stay in the temporary file and are checked by the program. Do not translate names from the source. Explain limitations in an analyst's language: not every fact is available in the file; execution and calculation correctness were not checked. Do not call the absence of observable variable names the absence of the variables themselves.

The document contains: file, date, and module count; the total and the five-level scale; the four directions in one row; the weight and contribution table; the conclusion, remarks, and recommendations; and the role and four rows of each module. Do not repeat the same rationales in separate sections. The final reply is a link and, when needed, one to three short sentences.
